# Investment Memo Template & Scoring Rubric

Applied to a company only once it's cleared a first-batch profile and looks like a serious thesis-fit candidate — not every tracked company gets a memo. Nine sections, then a weighted scorecard. The scorecard forces a number; the sections force the reasoning behind it.

---

## Sections

1. **Snapshot** — one paragraph: what they do, stage, last valuation, the one-sentence thesis for why this memo exists.
2. **Market / Problem** — what real problem this addresses, how big and how urgent, why now (not five years ago, not five years from now).
3. **Product & Tech** — what's actually built, how it works, what's genuinely hard about it (vs. what's a thin LLM wrapper).
4. **Team** — founders' relevant background, why they specifically are positioned to win this.
5. **Traction** — funding history, disclosed revenue/ARR, customers, growth rate — dated, sourced, no invented numbers.
6. **Competitive Landscape / Moat** — named competitors (incumbents and other startups), what actually defends this company's position, and what doesn't.
7. **Thesis Fit** — explicit scoring against the category thesis in `theses.md`, using its stated fit cues.
8. **Risks** — the honest list: market risk, regulatory risk, competitive risk, execution risk. A memo with no risks listed is a memo that wasn't actually interrogated.
9. **Scored Recommendation** — the scorecard below, the final verdict (watch / pass / revisit), and what would change the verdict.

## Scorecard

Five weighted categories, each scored 1-5 (1 = weak, 5 = exceptional). Weighted score = Σ(score × weight).

| Category | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Market timing | 20% | Is this the right window — not too early (no real demand yet), not too late (already commoditizing)? |
| Team | 20% | Do the founders have the specific unfair advantage to win this particular problem? |
| Wedge defensibility | 25% | Is the edge structural (proprietary data, regulatory moat, workflow lock-in) or copyable by an incumbent in a quarter? |
| Traction | 20% | Real, disclosed, dated evidence of product-market fit — not projections. |
| Thesis fit | 15% | How closely this matches the explicit scoring cues already written in `theses.md`, before this company was ever considered. |

**Reading the score:** ≥4.0 = strong watch, high-conviction follow. 3.0–3.9 = watch, worth continued tracking. 2.0–2.9 = revisit — real gaps, not yet convincing. <2.0 = pass, with the specific disqualifying reason stated.

The score is a forcing function for the writing, not a replacement for it — a 4.2 with weak reasoning behind it is worth less than a 3.4 with sharp reasoning.
